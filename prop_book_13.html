13   Blade chords ( before tip loss )

13.1   The blade chord for a given lift

Equation (11.8) gives the optimum thrust distribution including rotation. We will derive the corresponding blade chord. This chord does not yet include tip loss and viscous optimization. We will add those in later chapters.

  Equation (7.4) gave the thrust on a ring, and with the dm • of (7.8) for light loading, we have :

d T = d m • . V . b ( x ) = b ( x ) . ρ   V 2 . 2 π r . d r (13.1)

  The aerodynamic lift on the blade elements inside the ring must provide this thrust. The lift coefficient CL for a wing of area S is defined by :

L = C L . ½ ρ   V 2 .  S (13.2)

The blade area inside a ring is dS = B . c . dr, coming from B strips of width dr and chord c. The chord is defined in the direction of the local wind velocity W, as shown in the velocity diagram of figure 8.2. The lift generated by the total blade area dS within the ring is :

d L = C L .½ ρ   W 2 .  B .  c .  d r (13.3)

From figure 8.2, the local wind velocity W equals V / sin φ : x

d L = C L . ½  ρ .   V 2 sin 2 φ .  B .  c .  d r (13.4)

From the force diagram in figure 8.1, the thrust component of dL is :

d T = d L . cos φ (13.5)

We now have (13.1) for the ring thrust needed to generate an induction ratio b(x), and (13.5) for the ring thrust aerodynamically available from the blade elements to do this. The two must be equal :

ρ   . 2 π r . d r . b V 2 = C L . ½  ρ .   V 2 sin 2 φ .  B .  c .  d r . cos φ (13.6)

Dividing out the common factors ρ V 2 and dr, we have :

B . c 2π  r = 2 C L . b ( x ) . sin  φ . tan  φ (13.7)

The left hand term represents the fraction of the ring that is "covered by chord". We will call it the local solidity, but we have to note that the term "solidity" goes under many other definitions in the literature.

13.2   The non-dimensional solidity

Equation (2.10) defines x ≡ Ω r / V as the non-dimensional version of the radius r. We similarly define the non-dimensional chord c ′ :

  c^' ≡ Ω c⁄ V (13.8)

The ratio between c and r stays the same in the non-dimensional form. This will allow us to draw the blade planform (13.7) of c versus r in the same "true" proportions on a scale of c ′ versus x :

  c/ r = 〖 c〗^'/x (13.9)

The non-dimensional local solidity stays the same as in (13.7) :

  ( B .〖c 〗^')/ 2 π x= 2/C_L . b(x) .〖 sin〗⁡ϕ . 〖 tan〗⁡ϕ (13.10)

With (2.4) and (2.5) we can express this completely in x :

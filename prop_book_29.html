29   Tip sweepback

29.1   Tip sweepback

The increase of the lift force on a wing section with angle of attack acts at the quarter chord point. The classical planform of most wings, and many propeller blades therefore has a straight centerline at this percentage of chord.

  Even more than wings, propeller blade centerlines are subject to practical constraints such as bending moments, centrifugal forces, torsion, and aero-elasticity. And from the beginning there has also been a school of thought, maybe for reasons of fabrication, to keep the trailing edge straight. This typically gives the leading edges sweepback near the tips.

  The same straight trailing edges are seen traditionally in aircraft fins and stabilizers, and today in aircraft wings as well. There is some evidence that a straight trailing edge gives a flatter beginning of the wake sheet, and slightly less induced drag, than the 25% chord "straight" wing.

  In propellers, there may be a secondary advantage to the straight trailing edge. Even at moderate flying speeds, the blade tips can approach high Mach numbers with attendant noise and drag rise. Swept-back tips may help to alleviate these problems. In ships, they may help to shed cavitation bubbles.

  A simple, though not necessarily optimal way to achieve some tip sweepback is by applying the tip reduction K ( x ) one-sided from the trailing edge, instead of symmetrically around the centerline. This keeps the trailing edge as it was before the tip reduction, like in figure 27.3.

  The exact solution does not give K ( x ) as a separate factor, so we will use Prandtl's approximation F ′ ( x ) instead. With Prandtl's approximation, the chord comes from ( XXXXXXXXX ).

  Leaving out F ′(x) gives :

B . c  ′ = 4π CL . T′ κ G′ . cos 2 φ . sin  φ (29.1)

This is a larger outline than (28.1), with a larger thrust, and with a straight cropped tip as in figure 27.3. Before sweepback, it will envelop the shape (28.2) as reduced by K ( x ), c.q. F ′ ( x ).

  The Prandtl tip will modify the chords by a factor of F'(x). The difference with (28.2) will be :

  Δ(B .c^' )= ( F(x)-1 ) . 4 π/C_L . T^'/κ_( G^' ) 〖.cos〗^2 ϕ . sin⁡ϕ (29.2)

  Until now, we applied this difference symmetrically before and aft of the centerline. This will sweep the leading edge back by half, and the trailing edge forward by half. If we do not wish the trailing edge to sweep forward, then we will have to sweep back the centerline.

  We will call the sweepback &Deltay ′, defined as positive to the rear :

  〖Δ y〗^'= ½ .( 1-F'(x) ) . 1/B . 4 π/C_L . T^'/κ_( G^' ) 〖.cos〗^2 ϕ . sin⁡ϕ (29.3)
